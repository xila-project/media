# Not Your Regular

**Premise.** ALIX has hired a van, a forklift and a server room for the arrival of "the OS", and XILA turns up as a board that fits in their hand. Every cliché ALIX expects of an operating system (a big machine, a fat installer, one computer, one crash that takes everything down, being an OS at all) is flipped by a short, bratty and true answer from XILA, until the last joke reveals where the whole film has been happening.

**Hook.** _"Not your regular!" / "(O-S!)"_. That is five sung syllables with the stress on **NOT** and **REG**, plus a two-syllable gang shout. It is first planted as XILA's deadpan answer in the intro, then sung 13 times, then whispered at the very end.

**Why it's catchy.** It follows the energy mechanics of the reference without reusing its lines. There are 103 lines and 510 sung words in 2:50.6, which is about 3.0 words per second, across 14 short sections. The verses are rhymed rapid-fire couplets with two concrete images per line. The pre-chorus is a call-and-response that ends on a deflated punchline ("I rented a server room."). The post-chorus chant is built from 1–2 syllable shouts ("Board! Desk! Tab!"). The app verse escalates from a fern sending texts to a whole street of kazoos. There is a stop-time spoken break, a late-night infomercial parody bridge with a repeated tag ("Or open a tab."), a breakdown where an app crashes mid-solo, a final drop that is all hook, and a twist in the last 9 seconds. Every chorus rhymes on the "-ess" of _O-S_ (desk / yes / addresses / dressed / access / impressed), so the hook keeps echoing inside the lines.

**Why it sells the differentiator.** XILA's lines alone carry the whole message:

- **Small board:** _in your hand · 256 kibibytes of RAM · runs apps, plural · files and users_
- **Not a classic install:** _no discs, no wizard · core, drivers, apps: one binary · I'm a Rust library, build me in_
- **One app across three hosts:** _same app on a board, on a desk, in a browser tab · one signed package, three addresses_
- **Crashes stay small:** _the app did, I'm still here_
- **Open and easy to try:** _open source, you've got access · try me in your browser tab · Xila dot dev_

ALIX only ever sings the clichés.

**Film grammar.** The hub is a **loading dock** whose roller shutter is lettered NOT YOUR REGULAR. ALIX waits there with a yellow forklift. The film keeps leaving the dock for a different world per section:

- a cardboard moving-day set
- an empty rented server room
- a parcel depot with three address chutes
- a miniature felt-and-paper neighbourhood for the imagined apps
- a 4:3 late-night shopping channel
- a felt kazoo amphitheatre

The final pull-back shows that the dock is the back door of a public library. Words are built into the set: shutter lettering, address labels, a guest-list rope, and card-catalogue drawers labelled CORE / DRIVERS / APPS. Real artefacts serve as proof: the real board, the package lock entry, the Core repository, the Apache-2.0 notice, a genuine trap capture and the live browser demo. **XILA is never a robot character.** On screen, XILA is the real board, whose LED blinks on XILA's syllables, plus the official monogram used flat on the end card with its required clear space.

**Avoided signatures:** no comic-page transitions (we use physical door, shutter and whip-pan transitions), no amber/green agent terminal (the real Xila shell keeps its own look), no Anton (Montserrat only), no doubling call rooms, no window-spelled hook, no murmurations, no cream newspaper, no robot mascot, no purple sports car. No other OS is named or mocked, and nothing is compared on performance. The "regular OS" is only ALIX's own over-the-top moving-day ritual.

> **Claim correction (2026-11-18, director).** The Nano 33 BLE firmware is a headless Xila (serial shell, users, LittleFS, GPIO device files) and does **not** include the WebAssembly runtime (upstream `xila-project/platforms` `examples/nano_33_ble`). Lines that put apps on the board were rewritten: the board carries the **OS**; apps run on the desktop and in the browser tab (and the container image). Fictional apps on gadgets are introduced as imagined ("Imagined. For now."). Shot notes below that still mention apps on the board follow this correction.

## Fictional apps

All eight are **imagined**. They are built as felt, paper and practical props in a miniature neighbourhood, never dressed as screen captures. Each prop carries a small paper tag reading "imagined app". The end card and the video description say: **"Fern Pings, Cat Customs, Ding Critic, Toast Forecast, Snooze Court, Hill Snitch, Fridge Haiku and Kazoo Jam are imagined apps, made up to show what could run on Xila."** The song names them freely and never claims they exist.

## Self-critique (and fixes made)

- **Rhyme and density.** The earlier draft at this path was unrhymed, so it read like dialogue. Its app verse was random (sock trials, goose debts) and it never told a non-programmer _why_ those apps mattered. This version rhymes every verse couplet and every chorus line. Its verse lines run at about 8–10 syllables a bar, like the reference's verses. Each app line pairs a device with a one-word reaction, which gives two shots per line.
- **"Two-fifty-six kibibytes of RAM" has 9 syllables and is hard at 128 BPM.** It keeps a full bar and a ruler prop, and the stop-time repeats it dry and slow so the number lands once with no music under it.
- **Changed lines.** "kick keeps kicking, so's the rest" became "kick keeps going, I'm still dressed!" (a callback to "fully dressed", and it avoided a third "the rest"). Chorus 2's "open source, so read the rest" became "open source, you've got access!" for the same reason. The pre-chorus "Plural!" now pays off in the stop-time as "Imagined. For now."
- **Claims.** "The app did. I'm still here." is scoped to one observed trap; nothing is absolute. "Board, desk, tab" names three hosts and never says "everywhere". There are no speed or size comparisons and no other OS. The "regular OS" is only ALIX's own moving-day ritual. The board leg of "same app" and "apps on the board" needs a real board capture (note 1). This is the riskiest claim in the song, so it is flagged rather than hidden.
- **Remaining weak spots.** "Rusty! Open!" might be heard as "old and rusty". It is a Rust pun, and the stencil shot makes that clear; if it misfires in audition, swap in "Rust-built! Open!". "Free socks!" is pure absurdity with no payoff; it can become "Free rocks!" or be cut. The library twist depends on the set reveal. In audio alone it still works as a pun on "I'm a Rust library" from Verse 3.
- **Unverified.** No audio was generated and no credits were spent. Pronunciation of "kibibytes" and "Xila", duet consistency across 23 chunks, and whether the model honours "lead A / lead B" alternation all need an audition. If the alternation isn't honoured, the fallback is to split each mixed section into per-line chunks. That would exceed 30 chunks, so the two leads would be generated separately and edited together instead.
